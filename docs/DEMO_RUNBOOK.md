# DiastiMed RESOLVE · demonstration runbook

## Product claim

When an observation is unusable or equipment becomes unavailable, RESOLVE replans evidence collection and makes unresolved requirements explicit.

## Short judging demonstration

1. Load **Fatigue review**. Point to two usable observations and the missing context and vigilance evidence.
2. Select **Start check**, enter synthetic shift context and save. Show three usable observations and the reduced session allowance.
3. Disable **Workstation available**. Show the plan comparison: workstation task replaced by tablet task, 15 additional estimated seconds, and the device-comparison limitation.
4. Disable **Tablet available**. Show that vigilance remains unresolved; neither a complete review nor a medical clearance appears.
5. Open **Review brief** and export the packet. Show decision history, policy inputs and missing evidence.

For a 30-second recording, prepare the context entry in advance and narrate the same cause-and-effect sequence. Never imply a three-minute observation was performed during a short video. If showing a completed task, identify it as a previously recorded demonstration or use the explicitly synthetic complete scenario.

## Verification rehearsal

Ask an independent reviewer to identify the next check, explain why it is required, handle loss of both devices and identify the unresolved evidence in the review. Record completion, mistakes and their own explanation; do not invent study results. A small exercise is formative usability feedback, not validation.

## Evidence questions

- Why fatigue? NASA describes sleep disruption, workload and brief objective alertness observation as relevant research needs; linked primary sources are in Research & policy.
- Is the browser task validated? No. It is PVT-inspired and timing/quality rules are provisional.
- Does optimisation outperform greedy? Not in the current 169 paired complete cases. The current contribution is transparent collection control, constraint changes and reproducible handoff.
- Was it designed with astronauts? No astronaut co-design or operational evaluation has taken place.
- Does collection completeness mean safe to work? No. Human review and approved procedures remain necessary.
