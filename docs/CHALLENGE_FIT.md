# Challenge fit and award decision · 14 November 2026

Primary user hypothesis: a crew member assembling a fatigue-and-alertness review when equipment and routine time are limited. This is our design hypothesis, without astronaut interviews or co-design. The supported decision is which evidence can be collected next and what a human reviewer still needs. It does not determine fitness for duty.

The official 2026 challenge asks for gathering indicators and enabling astronauts to evaluate and act on health status. RESOLVE gathers self-reports and an experimental browser observation, explains acceptance and gaps, and prepares a bounded human-review handoff. The factual summary now separates observations, measurement limitations and the next review step. The gap remains substantial: there is no validated health assessment, diagnosis, autonomous treatment, complete emergency protocol or mission deployment. Four observations are a narrow demonstration, not coverage of every spaceflight health risk.

NASA support: HRP describes sleep disruption and workload risks. The Reaction Self Test record calls for brief objective observations with minimal burden. ExMC describes constraints on crew time, resources and communication. These motivate the problem and design. They do not establish our collection rules or approve the implementation.

Best Use of Technology remains the award hypothesis: demonstrate reliable evidence handling, execution guards, interruption recovery and reproducible handoff. A simple greedy planner ties RESOLVE in all 169 comparable complete cases. The finite exhaustive planner is retained because it is inspectable and independently checked; no superior optimisation performance is claimed. There is no evidence supporting an exclusive novelty or award guarantee. Best Use of Data is a weaker fit because no distinctive space-data analysis is implemented.
